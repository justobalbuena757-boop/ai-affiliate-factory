## You Can't Out-Train or Out-Diet a Sleep Deficit

Most people focus on exercise and diet when trying to improve their metabolism after 40 — but if you're not sleeping well, your best efforts will fall short.

The good news: specific nutrients can directly address the root causes of poor sleep after 40.

### 1. Magnesium (Glycinate Form)

Magnesium is involved in over 300 enzymatic reactions, including those that regulate sleep and stress.

**How it works**: Magnesium binds to GABA receptors, promoting relaxation and reducing cortisol. The glycinate form (chelated with glycine) also provides additional sleep-supporting benefits.

<StatCallout value="62%" label="improved sleep quality" variant="primary">
  A clinical trial found that magnesium supplementation improved sleep quality in 62% of older adults, reduced cortisol levels by 25%, and increased sleep time by 48 minutes per night.
</StatCallout>

### 2. L-Theanine

This amino acid, found naturally in green tea and also included in metabolic supplements like [Java Burn](https://www.javaburn.net/), is a powerhouse for sleep quality.

**How it works**: L-Theanine increases alpha brain waves (the same state produced by meditation), reduces anxiety without sedation, and counteracts the jittery effects of caffeine — making it a perfect partner for your morning coffee, not just before bed.

<InfoBox variant="tip" title="The Coffee Connection">
  L-Theanine is the reason why matcha drinkers report "calm alertness" instead of coffee jitters. Many metabolic supplements like Java Burn include L-Theanine specifically because it balances caffeine's stimulating effects while supporting deeper sleep at night.
</InfoBox>

### 3. Zinc

Zinc plays a critical role in sleep regulation and metabolic function.

**How it works**: Zinc is a key cofactor for melatonin synthesis. It also supports healthy testosterone levels, which naturally decline after 40 and directly affect sleep quality.

<StatCallout value="27%" label="faster sleep onset" variant="gradient">
  Studies show that zinc supplementation reduces the time to fall asleep by an average of 27% in adults with low baseline zinc levels.
</StatCallout>

### 4. Vitamin B6

B6 is essential for the production of both melatonin and serotonin — your sleep and mood regulators.

**How it works**: B6 is a cofactor in the conversion of tryptophan to serotonin, which is then converted to melatonin. Without adequate B6, this entire pathway stalls.

### 5. Glycine

This simple amino acid has a surprising effect on sleep quality.

**How it works**: Glycine lowers core body temperature (which signals your body it's time to sleep), improves sleep quality, and reduces next-day fatigue.

<StatCallout value="+25%" label="sleep quality improvement" variant="primary">
  A 500mg dose of glycine before bed has been shown to improve subjective sleep quality by 25% and reduce the time to fall asleep by 45%.
</StatCallout>

### 6. Vitamin D

The "sunshine vitamin" is surprisingly critical for sleep quality.

**How it works**: Vitamin D receptors are present in areas of the brain that regulate sleep. Low vitamin D is strongly correlated with sleep disorders, and over 40% of adults over 40 are deficient.

### 7. Apigenin (Chamomile)

This flavonoid promotes sleep by binding to benzodiazepine receptors in the brain.

**How it works**: Apigenin reduces anxiety, promotes relaxation, and supports the natural sleep cycle without the tolerance issues of pharmaceutical sleep aids.
