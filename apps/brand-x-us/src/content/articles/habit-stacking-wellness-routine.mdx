The most common reason wellness routines fail isn't lack of motivation — it's poor design. Habit stacking is a behavioral science technique that leverages your existing routines as anchors for new, healthy behaviors.

## What Is Habit Stacking?

Habit stacking, popularized by behavioral scientist BJ Fogg and author James Clear, is the practice of attaching a new habit to an existing one. The formula is simple:

> After [CURRENT HABIT], I will [NEW HABIT].

By linking a new behavior to something you already do automatically, you reduce the cognitive effort required to remember and initiate the new habit.

## Why Habit Stacking Works

### Existing Neural Pathways

Your brain has well-established neural pathways for existing habits. By piggybacking on these pathways, you bypass the resistance that comes with building entirely new routines.

### Contextual Triggers

Environmental cues are powerful habit triggers. When a new habit is consistently performed after an existing one in the same context, the association strengthens over time.

### Reduced Decision Fatigue

Every decision consumes mental energy. Habit stacking eliminates the decision of "when" and "where" to perform a new behavior, conserving willpower for other choices.

## Wellness Habit Stacks to Try

### Morning Hydration Stack
After [waking up], I will [drink a full glass of water with electrolytes].

### Mobility Stack
After [brushing my teeth], I will [do 30 seconds of neck and shoulder stretches].

### Metabolic Stack
After [pouring my morning coffee], I will [add collagen protein and cinnamon].

### Movement Stack
After [finishing my lunch], I will [walk for 10 minutes].

### Wind-Down Stack
After [turning off my phone for the night], I will [read for 15 minutes].

## How to Design Your Own Habit Stacks

**Step 1:** Identify three current habits you perform consistently (brushing teeth, making coffee, checking email)

**Step 2:** Choose one new health behavior you want to establish

**Step 3:** Pair the new behavior with an existing habit using the "After [current], I will [new]" formula

**Step 4:** Start with one stack for two weeks before adding another

## The Minimum Viable Habit

When designing a habit stack, make the new behavior so small it feels almost too easy. Five minutes of walking. One deep breath. One glass of water.

The key to habit formation isn't the size of the behavior — it's the consistency of the repetition.
